If \(D\lt0\), there are no real roots.
Why multiply by \(\sqrt2-1\)?
To get rid of the root in the denominator. The same trick will work with \(i\)!

Why do we need them?

Equations like \(x^2+1=0\) and \(x^2+4=0\) are not solvable in \(\mathbb R\): no real number has a negative square.

Euler was the first mathematician to introduce the symbol \(i\) (iota) for \(\sqrt{-1}\), with \(i^2=-1\). He also called it the imaginary number.

y = x² + 1 never touches the x-axis

So \(x^2=-1\) is simply impossible?
Only inside the real numbers. So we build a bigger system!

Meet iota: a new direction

I’m i. Real numbers live on one line. I bring a second direction, at 90°!

\(i^2=-1\). Now numbers like \(3+2i\) fill a whole plane.

What is a complex number?

\(\mathbb C=\{x+iy:\ x,y\in\mathbb R,\ i=\sqrt{-1}\}\)

e.g. \(5+3i,\ -1+i,\ 0+4i,\ 0+0i\)

\(x\) is the real part \(\operatorname{Re}(z)\), \(y\) is the imaginary part \(\operatorname{Im}(z)\). For \(z=3-4i\): \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=3\), \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=-4\).

Purely real: \(\operatorname{Im}(z)=0\).
Purely imaginary: \(\operatorname{Re}(z)=0\).
The sign \(+\) is not ordinary addition, and \(i\) is not a number to add. They are part of the scheme that writes the pair \((x,y)\) as one number.

The number family

Each set sits inside the next:

\(\mathbb N\subset\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R\subset\mathbb C\)

Every real number is complex: \(a=a+i0\).

\(x^2=-1\) has a solution only in \(\mathbb C\).

Equality and other rules

Equality. \(z_1=a_1+ib_1\) and \(z_2=a_2+ib_2\) are equal if \(a_1=a_2\) and \(b_1=b_2\), that is, \(\operatorname{Re}z_1=\operatorname{Re}z_2\) and \(\operatorname{Im}z_1=\operatorname{Im}z_2\).
No inequalities. \(a+ib\gt c+id\) is meaningful only if \(b=d=0\).
Careful with zero. For reals, \(a^2+b^2=0\Rightarrow a=b=0\). But for complex numbers \(z_1^2+z_2^2=0\) does not imply \(z_1=z_2=0\):
\(z_1=1+i,\ z_2=1-i:\ \ 2i+(-2i)=0\)
But if \(z_1z_2=0\), at least one of them must be 0, as for real numbers.

Square roots of negatives

For \(a\gt0\): \(\sqrt{-a}=\sqrt{-1}\,\sqrt a=i\sqrt a\).

Trap! \(\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}\) is valid only if at least one of \(a,b\) is non-negative. If both are negative, \(\sqrt a\sqrt b=-\sqrt{|a|\,|b|}\).
Illustration 1 \(\sqrt{-2}\sqrt{-3}=(i\sqrt2)(i\sqrt3)=i^2\sqrt6=-\sqrt6\)

Doubt corner

\(\sqrt{-2}\cdot\sqrt{-3}=\sqrt{(-2)(-3)}=\sqrt6\). Easy!
Careful! That rule needs at least one non-negative number. Convert to \(i\) first.
So \(\sqrt{-2}\sqrt{-3}=(i\sqrt2)(i\sqrt3)\)…
\(=i^2\sqrt6=-\sqrt6\). Not \(\sqrt6\)!

\(i^{4n}=1,\ i^{4n+1}=i,\ i^{4n+2}=-1,\ i^{4n+3}=-i\)

Sums of powers of i

Let \(f(m,n)=(i+i^2+i^3+\dots+i^n)^m\). Key fact: four consecutive powers add to 0, \(i^k+i^{k+1}+i^{k+2}+i^{k+3}=0\).

(i) \(f(4k,m)=0^m=0\)

(ii) \(f(4k+1,4p)=i^{4p}=1\)

(iii) \(f(4k+2,4p)+f(4k+3,p)=(i-1)^{4p}+(-1)^p=(-4)^p+(-1)^p\), purely real.

because \((i-1)^2=-2i\) and \(\left((-2i)^2\right)^p=(-4)^p\)

That looks scary!
Group four terms at a time. Each group of four powers adds to 0.

Value of a polynomial

If \(x=3+i\), find \(x^3-3x^2-8x+15\).

\(x-3=i\ \Rightarrow\ x^2-6x+10=0\)

\(x^3-3x^2-8x+15=x(x^2-6x+10)+3(x^2-6x+10)-15\)

\(=0+0-15=-15\)
Do I put \(x=3+i\) straight into \(x^3\)?
You can, but it is messy. Use \(x-3=i\), square it, and look for \(x^2-6x+10\).

Add and subtract

For \(z_1=a+ib,\ z_2=c+id\):

\((a+ib)+(c+id)=(a+c)+i(b+d)\)
\((a+ib)-(c+id)=(a-c)+i(b-d)\)

\((2+3i)+(1-i)=3+2i\)

Note. Addition and multiplication of complex numbers are commutative and associative. Multiplication is distributive over addition and subtraction.

Conjugate of a complex number

Definition. The conjugate of \(z=a+ib\) is \(\bar z=a-ib\): change the sign of the imaginary part.

\(\overline{3+i}=3-i,\quad \overline{2-5i}=2+5i,\quad \overline{7i}=-7i,\quad \bar 4=4\)

Key fact: a number times its conjugate is real.
\((a+ib)(a-ib)=a^2-i^2b^2=a^2+b^2\)

e.g. \((3+2i)(3-2i)=9+4=13\)

Divide: rationalise

\(\dfrac{a+ib}{c+id}\), where at least one of \(c,d\) is non-zero.

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate \(c-id\) of the denominator.

\(\dfrac{a+ib}{c+id}=\dfrac{(a+ib)(c-id)}{(c+id)(c-id)}\)
\(=\dfrac{ac+bd}{c^2+d^2}+i\,\dfrac{bc-ad}{c^2+d^2}\)

e.g. \(\dfrac{1+i}{1-i}=\dfrac{(1+i)^2}{2}=\dfrac{2i}{2}=i\)

Why multiply by the conjugate?
Because \((c+id)(c-id)=c^2+d^2\) is real. Same trick as rationalising surds!

Summary

Iota: \(i=\sqrt{-1},\ i^2=-1\). \(\mathbb C=\{x+iy:\ x,y\in\mathbb R\}\)
Powers: \(i^{4n}=1,\ i^{4n+1}=i,\ i^{4n+2}=-1,\ i^{4n+3}=-i\)
Equal: same real part and same imaginary part. No \(\gt\) or \(\lt\) between complex numbers.
Operations: add and subtract part by part; multiply by expanding with \(i^2=-1\); divide by multiplying by the conjugate.
Watch out: \(\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}\) needs one of \(a,b\) non-negative.
