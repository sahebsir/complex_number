A new question

We know \(\sqrt{-9}=3i\). But what is \(\sqrt{3+4i}\)?
Look for a number \(x+iy\) whose square is \(3+4i\). Try guessing!
\((2+i)^2=4+4i-1=3+4i\). And \((-2-i)^2\) works too: two roots!
Guessing will never work for \(7-24i\). Is there a method?
Yes. Let us build one.

Square root of a complex number

Let \(\sqrt{a+ib}=x+iy\), with \(x,y\) real. Squaring: \(x^2-y^2=a\) and \(2xy=b\).

\((x^2+y^2)^2=(x^2-y^2)^2+4x^2y^2=a^2+b^2\), so \(x^2+y^2=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).

\(x^2=\tfrac12\left[\sqrt{a^2+b^2}+a\right],\quad y^2=\tfrac12\left[\sqrt{a^2+b^2}-a\right]\)
Signs from \(2xy=b\): if \(b\gt0\), \(x\) and \(y\) have the same sign; if \(b\lt0\), opposite signs.

The formula

Let \(z=a+ib\) and \(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). From \(x^2=\tfrac12(|z|+a)\) and \(y^2=\tfrac12(|z|-a)\):

If \(b\gt0\):
\(\sqrt{a+ib}=\pm\left\{\sqrt{\dfrac{|z|+a}{2}}+i\sqrt{\dfrac{|z|-a}{2}}\right\}\)
If \(b\lt0\):
\(\sqrt{a+ib}=\pm\left\{\sqrt{\dfrac{|z|+a}{2}}-i\sqrt{\dfrac{|z|-a}{2}}\right\}\)

Check with \(3+4i\): \(|z|=5\), so \(\sqrt4+i\sqrt1=2+i\).

Doubt corner

Can I just write \(\sqrt{3+4i}=\sqrt3+i\sqrt4\)?
No! Square it: \((\sqrt3+2i)^2=-1+4\sqrt3\,i\), not \(3+4i\).
Why do we always get two answers?
Because \((-w)^2=w^2\). The two roots are \(w\) and \(-w\).

Let \(\sqrt{7-24i}=x+iy\). Squaring: \(x^2-y^2=7\) (1) and \(2xy=-24\) (2).

Summary

Method: set \(\sqrt{a+ib}=x+iy\). Squaring gives \(x^2-y^2=a\) and \(2xy=b\).
Then \(x^2+y^2=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\), so \(x^2=\tfrac12(|z|+a)\) and \(y^2=\tfrac12(|z|-a)\).
Signs: \(b\gt0\): \(x,y\) same sign. \(b\lt0\): opposite signs. Always two roots, \(w\) and \(-w\).
Quadratics: use \(z=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt D}{2a}\) and find \(\sqrt D\) with the method above.

Always check by squaring your answer.
